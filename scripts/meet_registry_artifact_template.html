What strength and tier mean, and what to watch out for

Strength — a 0-100 score, and it is a ranking against other meets, not against the record book. Built in four steps: (1) each athlete's form going into the race — an exponentially-weighted average of their earlier marks in that event — becomes a percentile among all performances in that event and era; (2) average that across the finalists in one event, giving a quality number for this meet's version of that event; (3) rank that number against every other meet contesting the same event in the same era; (4) average those per-event ranks across the meet. So 100 means "the strongest field of any meet running these events in this era" — the top of its peer group, not a field of world-record holders. That's why several meets sit at exactly 100.0: each is first in its own event-and-era grouping. It measures who showed up, not how fast the day was. Withheld ("—") below 5 scored events, except road races (a marathon only has one or two).

Strength (PB) — the previous basis, kept for comparison. It used each athlete's career-best mark instead of their form on the day. That had a leak: the career best was taken over an athlete's whole career, so it included marks set after the meet being scored — and often the mark set at it. A meet was partly scoring itself, which is why the fast record-setting courses sat highest and why every finalist at Berlin scored 99.2-99.4 under it: it could not tell the field apart. Changed 2026-09-03. Where the two columns disagree sharply, the fast courses fall and the tactical ones rise.

Tier — fixed by what the meet named itself for most classes (Olympics, Diamond League, age-group…). Road races are banded by strength (≥75 → T1, ≥50 → T2, else T3). Meets whose name matches no rule are never T1 and are split at the median-ish point of the unclassified field, recomputed each build — so a genuinely elite meet whose name we don't recognise is capped at T2. That cap is deliberate, but it does mean some T2 meets near the top of this list belong higher; the Doha Diamond League appears as "Doha Meeting" and is unclassified for exactly that reason.

Click any meet for its events: winning mark, that mark as a share of the world record (direction-aware, so it reads the same for times and distances), athletes in the final, and the event's own strength before averaging.

Known caveats — T1 counts are genuinely thin in several early years; that's corpus coverage, not a modelling artifact. Fixed 2026-09-03: strength moved from career-best to current form (see above); 407 meets were promoted out of a tier their own class rule forbade (the Greek, Irish, Belgian, Canadian, Swiss, Dutch and Brazilian national championships among them); three Diamond Leagues that had no name at all — Silesia, Athletissima Lausanne and Weltklasse Zürich, the DL Final — were named and moved to T1; and the asian_games class was split, so the South East Asian Games and a national race-walk grand prix are no longer classed as Asian Games. Earlier: the road-race reclassification bug (136 races promoted T2→T1) and the comp_name/comp_start backfill, both 2026-09-02/03.

Still known-wrong — class is a regex on the meet's name, so a meet named unusually falls to unclassified and is capped at T2 however strong it is. Real cases in this table: the Doha Diamond League ("Doha Meeting"), Grand Slam Track, Athlos NYC, and the United Airlines NYC Half, which is a road race not recognised as one. The World Athletics feed ships its own category code that would catch some of these, but it covers only 42.6% of meets and is multi-valued where it exists, so it is not yet used.
